# Saxenda (liraglutide) injection

Educational demo only. Not medical advice.

## What it's used for
Saxenda is a prescription medicine for weight management. Along with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity, it is used to help:
- adults with obesity, or adults with overweight who also have weight-related health problems, lose weight and keep it off;
- children aged 12 to 17 years who weigh more than 132 pounds (60 kg) and have obesity.
Saxenda is not used to treat type 2 diabetes (a different liraglutide product, Victoza, is used for diabetes).

## How it works
Saxenda contains liraglutide, which acts like the natural hormone GLP-1. It works on appetite areas of the brain to help you feel full sooner and less hungry, and it slows stomach emptying.

## Dosing schedule
Saxenda is injected once a day, every day. The dose is increased each week to lower stomach side effects:
- Week 1: 0.6 mg once daily
- Week 2: 1.2 mg once daily
- Week 3: 1.8 mg once daily
- Week 4: 2.4 mg once daily
- Week 5 and after: 3 mg once daily (maintenance dose)
For adults, if you have not lost at least 4% of your body weight after 16 weeks on 3 mg, your doctor may stop Saxenda because it is unlikely to work for you.

## How to take it
- Inject under the skin of your stomach area, thigh, or upper arm, once a day.
- You can take it at any time of day, with or without meals, but try to use it at about the same time each day.
- The pen is a multi-dose pen. Attach a new needle for each injection.
- Never share your pen with other people, even if the needle is changed.

## If you miss a dose
If you miss a daily dose, skip it and take your next dose at the usual time the next day.
Do not take an extra dose or a larger dose to make up for the missed dose.
If you miss more than 3 days in a row, call your doctor. You may need to restart at 0.6 mg to lower stomach side effects.

## Common side effects
The most common side effects are nausea, diarrhea, constipation, vomiting, injection site reactions, headache, low blood sugar, upset stomach, tiredness, dizziness, stomach pain, and changes in lab tests for the pancreas (lipase).
Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, low blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes, increased heart rate, kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, and depression or thoughts of suicide.

## Who should not take it
- People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC).
- People with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
- People who have had a serious allergic reaction to liraglutide or any ingredient in Saxenda.
- People who are pregnant or planning to become pregnant.
Saxenda has a boxed warning about possible thyroid C-cell tumors.

## Interactions and special situations
- Do not use with other liraglutide products (like Victoza) or other GLP-1 medicines.
- Diabetes medicines such as insulin or sulfonylureas: higher chance of low blood sugar.
- Pills taken by mouth: Saxenda slows stomach emptying, which may affect how other oral medicines are absorbed.
- Surgery or procedures: tell your care team before anesthesia or deep sedation.

## Storage
- Before first use: store in the refrigerator at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C).
- After first use: you can keep the pen at room temperature 59°F to 86°F (15°C to 30°C) or in the refrigerator for up to 30 days.
- Do not freeze. Do not use if it has been frozen.
- Keep the pen cap on to protect it from light, and remove the needle after each injection.
- Keep out of reach of children.
