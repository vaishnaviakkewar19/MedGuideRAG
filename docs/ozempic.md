# Ozempic (semaglutide) injection

Educational demo only. Not medical advice.

## What it's used for
Ozempic is a prescription medicine for adults with type 2 diabetes. It is used together with diet and exercise to help lower blood sugar.
It is also used to lower the risk of heart attack, stroke, or death from heart problems in adults with type 2 diabetes who already have heart disease.
It is also used to lower the risk of kidney disease getting worse in adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease [VERIFY].
Ozempic is not for people with type 1 diabetes. It is not approved for weight loss (a different semaglutide product, Wegovy, is used for weight management).

## How it works
Ozempic contains semaglutide, which acts like a natural hormone called GLP-1. It helps your pancreas release insulin when your blood sugar is high, lowers the amount of sugar your liver makes, and slows down how fast food leaves your stomach. Many people also feel less hungry.

## Dosing schedule
Ozempic is injected once a week, on the same day each week.
- Start with 0.25 mg once weekly for 4 weeks. This starting dose helps your body get used to the medicine; it is not meant to control blood sugar yet.
- Then increase to 0.5 mg once weekly.
- If more blood sugar control is needed after at least 4 weeks, your doctor may increase the dose to 1 mg once weekly.
- If still needed after at least 4 more weeks, the dose may be increased to 2 mg once weekly.
- The maximum dose is 2 mg once weekly.

## How to take it
- Inject under the skin (subcutaneously) of your stomach area (abdomen), thigh, or upper arm. Do not inject into a vein or muscle.
- You can take it at any time of day, with or without meals.
- Change (rotate) the injection spot each week.
- You may change the day of the week you inject, as long as your last dose was given at least 2 days (48 hours) before.
- Never share your pen with other people, even if the needle is changed.

## If you miss a dose
If you miss a dose, take it as soon as possible within 5 days after the missed dose.
If more than 5 days have passed, skip the missed dose and take your next dose on your regular day.
Do not take two doses to make up for a missed dose.

## Common side effects
The most common side effects are nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, stomach (abdominal) pain, and constipation. These are most common when you start or increase the dose and often get better over time.
Serious side effects can include inflammation of the pancreas (pancreatitis), gallbladder problems, low blood sugar (especially with insulin or a sulfonylurea), kidney problems from dehydration, serious allergic reactions, and changes in vision in people with diabetic eye disease (retinopathy).

## Who should not take it
- People who have had, or have family members who have had, a type of thyroid cancer called medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC).
- People with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
- People who have had a serious allergic reaction to semaglutide or any ingredient in Ozempic.
Ozempic has a boxed warning about possible thyroid C-cell tumors, seen in studies in rodents.

## Interactions and special situations
- Insulin or sulfonylureas: taking these with Ozempic raises the chance of low blood sugar. Your doctor may lower their dose.
- Pills taken by mouth: Ozempic slows stomach emptying, which may change how other oral medicines are absorbed.
- Do not use with other semaglutide products or other GLP-1 medicines.
- Pregnancy: stop Ozempic at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy [VERIFY].
- Surgery or procedures: tell your care team you take Ozempic before any procedure needing anesthesia or deep sedation, because food staying in the stomach can raise the risk of breathing in stomach contents.

## Storage
- Before first use: store in the refrigerator at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C).
- After first use: you can store the pen at room temperature 59°F to 86°F (15°C to 30°C) or in the refrigerator for up to 56 days.
- Do not freeze. Do not use if it has been frozen.
- Keep the pen cap on to protect it from light.
- Remove the needle after each injection and keep out of reach of children.
