# Mounjaro (tirzepatide) injection

Educational demo only. Not medical advice.

## What it's used for
Mounjaro is a prescription medicine for adults with type 2 diabetes. It is used with diet and exercise to help lower blood sugar.
Mounjaro is not for people with type 1 diabetes. It is not approved for weight loss (a different tirzepatide product, Zepbound, is used for weight management).

## How it works
Mounjaro contains tirzepatide. Tirzepatide acts like two natural hormones at the same time: GIP and GLP-1. Together they help your body release insulin when blood sugar is high, lower the sugar your liver makes, slow stomach emptying, and reduce appetite.

## Dosing schedule
Mounjaro is injected once a week.
- The starting dose is 2.5 mg once weekly for 4 weeks. This starting dose helps your body get used to the medicine; it is not meant to control blood sugar.
- After 4 weeks, increase to 5 mg once weekly.
- If more control is needed, the dose can be increased by 2.5 mg steps (7.5 mg, 10 mg, 12.5 mg, 15 mg) after at least 4 weeks on the current dose.
- The maximum dose is 15 mg once weekly.

## How to take it
- Inject under the skin of your stomach area, thigh, or upper arm, once a week on the same day.
- You can take it at any time of day, with or without meals.
- Mounjaro comes as a single-dose pen or single-dose vial [VERIFY].
- You may change your weekly injection day as long as there are at least 3 days (72 hours) between two doses.

## If you miss a dose
If you miss a dose, take it as soon as possible within 4 days (96 hours) after the missed dose.
If more than 4 days have passed, skip the missed dose and take your next dose on your regular day.
Do not take two doses within 3 days of each other.

## Common side effects
The most common side effects are nausea, diarrhea, decreased appetite, vomiting, constipation, upset stomach (indigestion), and stomach pain.
Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, low blood sugar (especially with insulin or a sulfonylurea), serious allergic reactions, kidney problems from dehydration, severe stomach problems, vision changes in people with diabetic eye disease, and gallbladder problems.

## Who should not take it
- People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC).
- People with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
- People who have had a serious allergic reaction to tirzepatide or any ingredient in Mounjaro.
Mounjaro has a boxed warning about possible thyroid C-cell tumors.

## Interactions and special situations
- Birth control pills: Mounjaro may make birth control pills taken by mouth work less well, because it slows stomach emptying. Use a non-oral method (or add a barrier method like condoms) for 4 weeks after starting Mounjaro and for 4 weeks after each dose increase.
- Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher chance of low blood sugar. Your doctor may lower their dose.
- Other pills taken by mouth may be absorbed differently.
- Do not use with other tirzepatide products (like Zepbound) or GLP-1 medicines.
- Pregnancy: tell your doctor if you are pregnant or plan to become pregnant.
- Surgery or procedures: tell your care team before anesthesia or deep sedation.

## Storage
- Store in the refrigerator at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C).
- If needed, each single-dose pen or vial can be kept out of the refrigerator at up to 86°F (30°C) for up to 21 days.
- Do not freeze. Do not use if it has been frozen.
- Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- Keep out of reach of children.
