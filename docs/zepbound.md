# Zepbound (tirzepatide) injection

Educational demo only. Not medical advice.

## What it's used for
Zepbound is a prescription medicine for weight management. Along with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity, it is used:
- to help adults with obesity, or adults with overweight who also have weight-related health problems, lose weight and keep it off;
- to treat moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in adults with obesity.
Zepbound is not used to treat type 2 diabetes on its own (a different tirzepatide product, Mounjaro, is used for diabetes).

## How it works
Zepbound contains tirzepatide, which acts like two natural hormones: GIP and GLP-1. It works on appetite areas of the brain to help you feel full and eat less, and it slows down how fast food leaves your stomach.

## Dosing schedule
Zepbound is injected once a week.
- Start with 2.5 mg once weekly for 4 weeks. This starting dose is for getting used to the medicine.
- Increase by 2.5 mg every 4 weeks until you reach your maintenance dose.
- The maintenance dose is 5 mg, 10 mg, or 15 mg once weekly, depending on what your doctor decides [VERIFY].
- The maximum dose is 15 mg once weekly.

## How to take it
- Inject under the skin of your stomach area, thigh, or upper arm, once a week on the same day.
- You can take it at any time of day, with or without meals.
- Zepbound comes as a single-dose pen or single-dose vial.
- You may change your weekly injection day as long as there are at least 3 days (72 hours) between two doses.

## If you miss a dose
If you miss a dose, take it as soon as possible within 4 days (96 hours) after the missed dose.
If more than 4 days have passed, skip the missed dose and take your next dose on your regular day.
Do not take two doses within 3 days of each other.

## Common side effects
The most common side effects are nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation, stomach pain, indigestion, injection site reactions, tiredness, allergic reactions, belching, hair loss, and heartburn.
Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, low blood sugar (in people with type 2 diabetes), serious allergic reactions, kidney problems, severe stomach problems, depression or thoughts of suicide, and eye problems in people with diabetes.

## Who should not take it
- People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC).
- People with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
- People who have had a serious allergic reaction to tirzepatide or any ingredient in Zepbound.
Zepbound has a boxed warning about possible thyroid C-cell tumors.

## Interactions and special situations
- Birth control pills: Zepbound may make birth control pills taken by mouth work less well. Use a non-oral method (or add a barrier method like condoms) for 4 weeks after starting Zepbound and for 4 weeks after each dose increase.
- Do not use with other tirzepatide products (like Mounjaro) or GLP-1 medicines.
- Diabetes medicines such as insulin or sulfonylureas: higher chance of low blood sugar.
- Pregnancy: do not use Zepbound while pregnant, because weight loss offers no benefit during pregnancy [VERIFY].
- Surgery or procedures: tell your care team before anesthesia or deep sedation.

## Storage
- Store in the refrigerator at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C).
- If needed, each single-dose pen or vial can be kept out of the refrigerator at up to 86°F (30°C) for up to 21 days.
- Do not freeze. Do not use if it has been frozen.
- Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- Keep out of reach of children.
