# GLP-1 medicines: a general overview

Educational demo only. Not medical advice.

## What are GLP-1 medicines
GLP-1 medicines are a group of prescription medicines that act like a natural gut hormone called GLP-1. People use them for type 2 diabetes or for weight management. They can help with blood sugar, appetite, and weight. Some people take them every week as an injection, some take them every day as an injection, and one is a daily tablet. If you miss a dose, the right thing to do depends on which medicine you take.

## The medicines in this family
- Semaglutide is the ingredient in Ozempic (for diabetes), Wegovy (for weight), and Rybelsus (a tablet for diabetes).
- Tirzepatide is the ingredient in Mounjaro (for diabetes) and Zepbound (for weight). It also acts on a second hormone called GIP.
- Liraglutide is the ingredient in Victoza (for diabetes) and Saxenda (for weight).
Medicines with the same ingredient can have different doses, schedules, and rules. Always read the guide for your exact medicine.

## How people usually take them
Most of these medicines are injections under the skin with a pen. Some are used once a week and some once a day. The tablet form has its own special rules about food and water. Storage rules differ too: some pens need the refrigerator and some can stay at room temperature for a while. Ask your pharmacist about missed doses, storage, and how to take your medicine with meals.

## Things people often ask about
People often ask about stomach side effects like nausea, about missed doses, about travel and keeping pens cold, about birth control, about other medicines, and about pregnancy. The answers are different for each medicine, so this overview does not give specific doses, timings, or rules.

## Talking with your care team
Tell your doctor about all medicines you take, any history of thyroid cancer or pancreas problems, and any plans for pregnancy or surgery. Your care team can explain the dose and schedule that is right for you.
