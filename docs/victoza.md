# Victoza (liraglutide) injection

Educational demo only. Not medical advice.

## What it's used for
Victoza is a prescription medicine used with diet and exercise to lower blood sugar in adults and children aged 10 years and older with type 2 diabetes.
It is also used to lower the risk of heart attack, stroke, or death from heart problems in adults with type 2 diabetes and known heart disease.
Victoza is not for people with type 1 diabetes and is not approved for weight loss (a different liraglutide product, Saxenda, is used for weight management).

## How it works
Victoza contains liraglutide, which acts like the natural hormone GLP-1. It helps your pancreas release insulin when blood sugar is high, lowers the sugar your liver makes, and slows stomach emptying.

## Dosing schedule
Victoza is injected once a day, every day.
- Start with 0.6 mg once daily for 1 week. This starting dose helps reduce stomach side effects; it is not meant to control blood sugar.
- After 1 week, increase to 1.2 mg once daily.
- If more control is needed, the dose may be increased to 1.8 mg once daily.
- The maximum dose is 1.8 mg once daily.

## How to take it
- Inject under the skin of your stomach area, thigh, or upper arm, once a day.
- You can take it at any time of day, with or without meals, but try to use it at about the same time each day.
- The pen is a multi-dose pen. Attach a new needle for each injection.
- Never share your pen with other people, even if the needle is changed.

## If you miss a dose
If you miss a daily dose, skip it and take your next dose at the usual time the next day.
Do not take an extra dose or a larger dose to make up for the missed dose.
If you miss more than 3 days in a row, call your doctor. You may need to restart at 0.6 mg to lower stomach side effects.

## Common side effects
The most common side effects are nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, decreased appetite, upset stomach, and constipation.
Serious side effects can include pancreatitis, low blood sugar (especially with insulin or a sulfonylurea), kidney problems, serious allergic reactions, and gallbladder problems.

## Who should not take it
- People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC).
- People with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).
- People who have had a serious allergic reaction to liraglutide or any ingredient in Victoza.
Victoza has a boxed warning about possible thyroid C-cell tumors.

## Interactions and special situations
- Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher chance of low blood sugar. Your doctor may lower their dose.
- Pills taken by mouth: Victoza slows stomach emptying, which may affect how other oral medicines are absorbed.
- Do not use with other liraglutide products (like Saxenda) or other GLP-1 medicines.
- Pregnancy: tell your doctor if you are pregnant or plan to become pregnant.
- Surgery or procedures: tell your care team before anesthesia or deep sedation.

## Storage
- Before first use: store in the refrigerator at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C).
- After first use: you can keep the pen at room temperature 59°F to 86°F (15°C to 30°C) or in the refrigerator for up to 30 days [VERIFY].
- Do not freeze. Do not use if it has been frozen.
- Keep the pen cap on to protect it from light, and remove the needle after each injection.
- Keep out of reach of children.
